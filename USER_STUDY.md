## Conclusion

Version A (live) was faster to complete on average (9.24s vs. 11.98s for Version B), but Version B scored higher on both perceived responsiveness (4.1 vs. 3.6) and comfort (4.5 vs. 4.0). Error-correction counts were similar across both (0.8 vs. 0.6 average), suggesting Version A's speed advantage didn't come from participants making fewer mistakes. This points to a real trade-off rather than one version being simply "better": Version A feels and is objectively quicker, but its continuously updating text appears to cost a small amount of perceived calm compared to Version B's single, stable reveal. Which matters more depends on use case — Version A for quick dictation under time pressure, Version B for a tool meant to feel deliberate and trustworthy.
